# Star Spin feedback (1.7)

Uses docs/game-feedback.md. Every reel stop, nudge and amount comes from the accepted server spin. The displayed stack is one three-cell tower whose position follows the recorded cyclic stop path. No decorative event changes a wager or future result. Version 1.7 keeps this event order and adds stronger, still event-bound visuals and original synthesized saber and Force sounds.

| Event | Visible meaning and phases | Sound and synchronization | Interruption and verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pending request | Shock ring from the reactor button, a charge flying to the reels, light streaks in blurred strips, faster frame ring and conduits; status pending | Saber ignition and a low hum while the reels turn; no result cue | Stop cancels autoplay, not an accepted request. Unknown replies retain the common pending UUID. |
| Accepted stop | The actual pre-nudge window lands left to right with a short rebound, contact glow, ring and sparks; SCATTER portals start turning | One low contact per reel on the host output timeline; the hum retracts on the fifth reel | Reduced motion reveals directly. Closing, pause and skip cancel owned sources, canvas particles and motion. |
| Dovetail step | One raw strip cell advances in the confirmed direction; lightning into the tower, a wave up the column, multiplier pops with a ring | Saber clash with crackle; one shared visual/audio accent per actual step | Only the recorded steps are shown. No whole-interface shake. Light flashes are rate limited to fewer than three per second, also in quick play. |
| Bonus entry | Portals grow, gold lightning to the centre, a vortex and one flash; the hall turns amber; eight segments light one by one | Force swell with a choir, then a clash and a short brass call; music ducks | Restored bonus shows the current remaining count quietly, without the entry sequence. |
| Paid line | Three-layer beam with a travelling light point; paid symbols step forward on rays, other cells dim; several lines take turns afterwards | Saber swing with a thin shimmer | Lines below the stake stay a thin silver line, without rays or dimming. |
| Positive result | "Win" banner counting up to the confirmed total, a star fountain, a flash behind the companion and a ring at her feet | Saber swing and a three-note brass motif | No banner for a smaller-than-stake return, an exact return or a recovered result. |
| Big result, from 20 stakes | One flash, hyperspace streaks across the pane, the hangar background zooms briefly, a large banner counts up, gold rain | Rising hyperspace sweep, low boom, timpani roll, clash and a brass chord; music ducks | The threshold is a game design hypothesis, not a measured perception limit. Skip shows the exact total at once. |
| Bonus end | "Overdrive complete" banner with the total for the whole paid round | Brass finale for a positive round, quiet tone otherwise | Plain banner without rays when the total is not above the stake. |
| Side change | A light sweep crosses the pane while colours change | The old blade retracts, the new one ignites: Light is cleaner and higher, Dark lower and driven | Not available during an accepted bonus or a request. |

The visual lead, short stop intervals, nudge duration, banner holds and the 20-stake threshold are game-specific art choices. Scheduling records host outputTimestamp/outputLatency estimates and measured visual skew. These are engineering observations on the test browser, not a claim about physical Bluetooth or all devices. Slow main-thread handling and hidden pages must not release stale cue bursts.

Music is an original rendered ambient space score, not the Star Wars soundtrack. Effects are synthesized in the page through the same host context and output: detuned sawtooth blades through a resonant filter, filtered noise, inharmonic metal partials, formant choir and brass-like saws. Nothing is sampled from films. In-game music/effects switches are independent, respect the common mute/volume, and persist. Pausing and closing stop all owned audio. Pleasantness and fatigue require user listening and physical-device checks; signal measurements alone do not establish them.
